# Networth glossary and teaching voice

The writer loads this with knowledge/PRODUCT.md. Rows and patterns are paraphrased from the six
beginner videos in docs/research/research-beginner-videos.md. The IPO section at the end is
Networth's own wording, because the videos never cover those terms. Use a term's plain words in
the same sentence as the term. Never turn a row into advice.

## Terms

| Concept | Plain English | Hinglish |
|---|---|---|
| Stock / share / equity | A small slice of a company you own. | Company ka chhota sa hissa, jisse aap uske part-owner ban jaate ho. |
| Stock market | A digital marketplace where people trade those slices. | Ek online bazaar jahan shares trade hote hain. |
| Demat account | The account that holds your shares electronically, like a locker for them. | Demat matlab shares ka digital locker; bank account jaisa, par shares ke liye. |
| PAN card | Tax ID you need to open the account. | Demat khulwane ke liye PAN zaroori hai. |
| Broker / brokerage | The app or company that places your orders. | Woh app jisse aap order lagate ho (jaise Zerodha, Groww type apps). |
| Brokerage vs retirement account | Account types; tax-advantaged ones (ISA, Roth IRA, 401k) are US/UK. | India mein yeh nahi, yahan demat + mutual fund folio chalta hai. |
| Index | A scoreboard that tracks a fixed group of companies. | Index = badi companies ki list ka score, jaise Nifty 50. |
| S&P 500 | Index of about 500 big US companies. | (US item) |
| Nifty 50 / Sensex | India's headline indexes of large companies. | Nifty 50 = India ki top 50 badi companies ka index. |
| Index fund | A fund that just copies an index, so you own all its companies in one go. | Index fund = ek hi investment mein poore index ki companies. |
| Mutual fund | A pooled basket of stocks run by a professional manager. | Mutual fund = ek tokri jisme kai companies ke shares, manager sambhalta hai. |
| Active vs passive | Manager picks stocks vs fund just copies an index. | Active mein manager chunta hai, passive mein index ko copy karte hain. |
| ETF | A basket of companies that trades on the exchange like a single stock. | ETF mein basket hota hai par price din bhar stock jaise badalta hai. |
| SIP | Investing a fixed small amount on a schedule instead of all at once. | SIP = har mahine thoda-thoda paisa, ek tareeka hai, koi alag product nahi. |
| Lump sum | Investing everything at once. | Lumpsum = ek baar mein poora paisa. |
| Dollar-cost / rupee-cost averaging | Investing regularly gets you more units when cheap, fewer when dear, smoothing your average price. | Rupee cost averaging: sasta ho to zyada units, mehenga ho to kam. |
| Compounding | Gains earn gains, so growth speeds up over time. | Byaj par byaj, time ke saath paisa tezi se badhta hai. |
| Rule of 72 | Divide 72 by the yearly return to estimate years to double. | 72 ko return se divide karo, utne saal mein paisa double. |
| Time in the market / long term | Holding for years beats guessing entry points. | Lambe time tak ruko; timing ki jagah time dena zyada kaam karta hai. |
| Diversification | Spreading money so one failure does not sink you. | Saara paisa ek jagah nahi; alag-alag companies mein baanto. |
| Risk / risk appetite | How much ups and downs you can stomach. | Risk appetite = kitni girawat aap bardaasht kar sakte ho. |
| Volatility | How much the price jumps around. | Price ka upar-neeche hona. |
| Time horizon | How long until you need the money. | Paisa kitne saal baad chahiye. |
| Emergency fund | 3 to 6 months of expenses kept aside before investing. | Investment se pehle 3-6 mahine ka kharcha alag rakho. |
| Insurance (health, term) | Protection so one event does not wreck savings. | Health aur term insurance pehle, phir stock market. |
| Delivery vs intraday | Holding shares vs trading in and out the same day. | Delivery = rakhna; intraday = usi din nikalna (zyada risky). |
| Technical vs fundamental analysis | Reading charts vs reading company health. | Chart dekhna vs company ke numbers dekhna. |
| Bull / bear | Optimistic (prices up) vs pessimistic (prices down). | Bull = tezi, bear = mandi. |
| Bubble / frothy | Prices inflated well beyond what companies justify. | Bhaav fule hue, phat sakte hain. |
| Market cap | Share price times number of shares: the company's size. | Company ki kul keemat; bada = large cap. |
| Large / mid / small / micro cap | Size buckets, generally bigger = steadier. | Large cap steady, mid/small cap zyada upar-neeche. |
| Blue-chip / penny stock | Big established name vs very cheap tiny speculative one. | Blue-chip = bade bharosemand naam; penny stock = bahut risky. |
| Sector / theme | Industry group (IT, energy) or story group (AI, ESG). | Sector = industry ka group, jaise IT, banking. |
| IPO | A company first offering shares to the public to raise money. | Company jab pehli baar shares public ko bechti hai. |
| Dividend | Share of profit paid out to owners. | Company ke munafe ka hissa shareholders ko. |
| Accumulation vs distribution | Fund reinvests dividends vs pays them out. | Reinvest hota hai ya cash aata hai. |
| Expense ratio / fees | Yearly fee a fund charges; index funds are low. | Fund ki saalana fees; index fund mein kam. |
| P/E ratio | Price paid per 1 of yearly profit; compare within the same industry. | Company ke har rupaye ke munafe ke liye aap kitna dete ho. |
| Price-to-sales, free cash flow | Valuation without profit; cash left after running costs. | Bina profit wali company ka mapdand; bacha hua cash. |
| Balance sheet / income statement / cash-flow statement | The three company reports: what it owns vs owes, earnings, cash movement. | Company ki teen report: kya hai, kitna kamaya, cash kaise aaya-gaya. |
| Revenue / net income / margin | Total sales / profit after all costs / profit share of sales. | Kul bikri / asli munafa / munafe ka percent. |
| Debt, return on equity | How much a company owes; profit made per unit of owners' money. | Company ka karz; owners ke paise par kamai. |
| Inflation | Prices rising, so idle cash gets you less each year. | Mehngai; bank mein padi cash ki keemat ghat'ti hai. |
| Savings vs investing returns | Savings accounts pay little; stocks have paid more historically (not promised). | Savings par kam byaj, stocks par historically zyada, pakki nahi. |
| Market order / limit order | An order at the current price vs only at a price you set. | Market order = abhi ke bhaav par; limit = apne tay bhaav par. |
| Ticker symbol | Short code identifying a stock or fund. | Share ka short code. |
| Capital gains / tax-loss harvesting | Tax on profit when you exit; offsetting gains with losses. | Nikalne par munafe par tax (India ke rules alag, short/long term). |
| Target-date fund, bonds, three-fund portfolio | Fund that shifts safer over time; loans to governments/companies; stock+international+bond mix. | (Mostly US; bonds = sarkar ko udhaar dena.) |
| Rebalancing | Bringing the mix back to target proportions. | Mix ko dobara tay hisse par laana. |
| Don't panic when markets fall | Dips are normal; exiting in fear locks losses. | Girawat mein ghabrao mat. |

## IPO and company-filing terms

| Concept | Plain English | Hinglish |
|---|---|---|
| DRHP / RHP | The draft (and then final) document a company files with SEBI before an IPO, describing its business, money and risks. | IPO se pehle SEBI ko di gayi company ki poori kundli. |
| Price band | The lowest and highest price per share the company will accept for the IPO. | IPO mein ek share ki kam se kam aur zyada se zyada keemat. |
| Lot size | The fixed number of shares in each bid. | Ek baar mein kitne shares ka bid lagana padta hai. |
| Issue size | The total money the IPO raises, in crore. | IPO se company kul kitna paisa uthayegi. |
| Fresh issue | New shares; the money goes to the company. | Naye shares; paisa company ke paas jaata hai. |
| Offer for sale (OFS) | Existing owners offloading their shares; the money goes to them, not the company. | Purane maalik apne shares de rahe hain; paisa unke paas jaata hai. |
| Grey market premium (GMP) | An unofficial, unregulated guess of the listing price, traded outside the exchange. Not reliable. | Bina niyam wala andaaza, bharosemand nahi. |
| Anchor investors | Large institutions that commit money a day before the IPO opens. | Bade sansthan jo IPO khulne se pehle paisa lagate hain. |
| Subscription (e.g. 3x) | How many times the shares on offer were bid for. | Jitne shares the, unse kitne guna bid aaye. |
| Listing date | The day the shares start trading on NSE and BSE. | Jis din shares exchange par trade hona shuru hote hain. |
| Allotment | Who actually gets shares when bids exceed shares on offer, decided by lottery for small bids. | Kisko shares mile, chhote bids mein lottery se. |
| Revenue / PAT | Total sales / profit after tax: what is left after every cost and tax. | Kul bikri / tax ke baad bacha munafa. |
| EBITDA | Earnings before interest, tax and accounting write-downs; a rough view of operating profit. | Byaj, tax aur ghisaai se pehle ki kamai. |
| Order book | Work a company has been contracted for but not yet delivered or billed. | Mile hue kaam jo abhi poore nahi hue. |
| Market cap at the upper band | The company's total value if every share were priced at the top of the band. | Upar wale bhaav par poori company ki keemat. |
| Promoter | The founders or group that control the company. | Company chalane wale maalik. |
| Lock-in | A period when certain holders may not exit their shares. | Kuch samay tak shares nahi nikal sakte. |

## Teaching patterns

1. **One thread, one idea, one next step.** V6 runs a single "company offers slices" story and builds stock, fund, ETF and SIP from it. V5 gives a four-beat plan and ends on a single action. A WhatsApp reply should carry exactly one idea and offer one follow-up.
2. **Ladder of definitions.** Define the new thing as "like the previous thing, except X" (ETF = fund that trades like a stock). Keeps each message to two or three sentences.
3. **Everyday object analogies.** Music charts for an index, a closet for a demat account, a basket for a mutual fund, buckets and cups for diversification, supermarket price tag vs haggling at a market for market vs limit order, airport codes for tickers, a first date for reading financial statements. Pick local ones for India (kirana, chai, auto fare).
4. **Small round numbers, shown twice.** Compounding: 1,000 at 10% becomes 1,100 then 1,210. Averaging: three monthly prices and the resulting average. Doubling: Rule of 72 with Rs 1 lakh. Always show the arithmetic in one line, then the takeaway in one more.
5. **Price the habit as a coffee.** Daily Rs 5 (one coffee) makes a tiny recurring amount feel painless. For India use a chai or auto ride.
6. **Order of ideas that worked:** why bother (inflation, idle cash) then what a stock is then risk and diversification then product types then how to start (account) then do not panic. Safety-net first (emergency fund, insurance) appears in V1 and V2.
7. **Fear first.** Open with the worry (will I lose money, is it too late) and answer it with time, not with tips (V1, V2, V3).
8. **Remember-it hooks.** Bulls have horns pointing up, bears have claws swinging down. Cheap mnemonics survive in a chat.
9. **Compare within a group.** "A ratio only means something against similar companies." Good template for explaining any number.
10. **Disclaimers inline, short.** V2 and V3 say once, in plain words, that this is not personal advice and returns can fall. Keep it to one clause.
11. **Hinglish register (V5, V6).** English for terms (stock, ETF, SIP, risk), Hindi for connecting words and feelings. Mirror the user's language each turn.
12. **Short-format (Shorts) notes.** About 100 to 150 spoken words. A hook in the first sentence that names who it is for. Beats are numbered or clearly sequenced. A joke or relatable aside per beat to keep attention. A concrete "do one thing" close. A soft call to action (reply with a word to get more). Strip nuance rather than compress it, and say "ask me for the details" to push depth into the next turn. Danger: brevity encourages absolute claims (best, safe, at least 10 percent), which Networth must not copy.
