Every part we build, how it works, and the order we build it in over 24 hours. Read with the product spec. One rule runs through all of it: the engine computes every number, and the AI only writes words around them.

Two kinds of Claude. Claude Code, on Ankit's plan, does all build-time thinking: writing the code, curating the event library, running backtests, the improvement loop and the evals. The product has to keep running when the laptop is closed and serve other people, so it can't sit on a personal Claude Code plan.

02

Architecture

One VM does the thinking. Keiki does the talking. Everything the user sees traces back to the engine box.

Maritime VM

A Docker container that listens on $PORT, answers GET /health and POST /chat within 30 seconds, and keeps everything in /data. Maritime cron triggers wake it daily for investing-day briefs and every 15 minutes in Indian and US market hours.

Keiki

Handles the Telegram and WhatsApp conversations, per-user memory and forwarded messages. It calls our /tools/* endpoints as webhook tools. We push briefs with its notice API.

Offline

The backtest runs once at build time, then nightly. It reads the price store and the event library and writes the base-rate table. Nothing user-facing waits on it.

How one event becomes a million different messages

Shared, computed once per event. Base rates per (event type, asset) come from the engine and are cached. A million users don't mean a million backtests.

Filter per user. Drop any asset in exclude[] and any market not in markets[].

Order per user. The scoreboard is the same for everyone. What leads changes with the goal: a 3-year house goal hears about drops and recovery first, a 25-year horizon hears about long-run history.

Size per user. Ranges shown on their monthly_amount, in rupees or dollars, as illustrations. Their own plan is quoted back, never changed by us.

Compose per user. One Sonnet call with the profile in context. At large scale the five lines become a template filled from the engine, and the model handles only chat. That keeps cost roughly flat per user.

✓

Why numbers_used exists. Every message records which engine values it quoted. The guardrail checks that every number in the text appears in that list. That is how "every call shows its work" becomes something the code enforces.

04

The engine

An event study, the standard method from finance research. For each past event, measure what each asset did against its own market (the S&P for US assets, the Nifty 50 for Indian ones) over a fixed window, then summarize honestly.

Rules it enforces
  • No peeking. An event after 4pm ET counts from the next session. A leak test fails the build if any return uses future data.
  • Episodes. Events within 21 trading days merge into one episode. Odds count episodes.
  • Baseline. Hit rate in 1,000 random windows of the same length, next to the event hit rate.
  • Two eras. Since 1926 from industry portfolios, since 1998 from ETFs. Both are shown when they disagree.
  • Weak signal under 8 episodes, in words and numbers.
The odds API
GET /tools/odds?type=oil_supply&asset=JETS
→ { n_episodes: 9, hit_rate: .73,
    baseline: .49, median: -.024,
    p10: -.09, p90: .03, era: "since_1998",
    episodes: [...] }

GET /tools/position_range
    ?user=u_1&ticker=JETS&type=oil_supply
→ { value: 2000, p10: -180,
    median: -48, p90: 60 }
!

The outside check. Before we trust any number, the engine must reproduce a published result. The candidate is Lucca and Moench (2015), who report that the S&P tends to rise in the 24 hours before scheduled Fed announcements. That's cited from memory, so step one is to confirm it in the paper. If our engine, run on SPY and real FOMC dates, can't show the effect, the engine has a bug.

05

News watcher and triggers

This replaces the old "paste a headline" idea. The system finds the stories itself.

Pull

Every 15 minutes in NSE hours (9:15 to 15:30 IST) and US hours, hourly otherwise. Exa news search over the last 48 hours with queries per event type and per asset held by any user. GDELT as backup.

Group and confirm

Haiku groups articles into stories by title and summary. A story is confirmed with 3 or more original publishers in 24 hours, or 1 primary source such as the Fed, SEC, RBI, SEBI, PIB, NSE filings, OPEC or a company's investor page. Indian publishers include Economic Times, Mint, Business Standard and Moneycontrol. Wire reposts count once.

Trigger

Price first. An asset's excess return today beyond 2.5 times its 60-day volatility, then find the confirmed story behind it. News first. A confirmed story that classifies into a known type and cause.

Every story that fails a check is still logged with the reason. That log is the "honesty moment" in the demo, and it's how we tune the thresholds.

Agents and skills

Spec-driven and skill-driven. An agent is a spec file. A skill is a folder with a SKILL.md. The harness turns a spec into a running agent with only the skills and tools it lists.

→

Shared product knowledge. The harness loads knowledge/PRODUCT.md into every agent's system prompt, first and unchanged, so it stays cache-stable across calls. It holds who we are, the line we never cross, trust rules and voice. If a spec or skill conflicts with it, that file wins.

Evals and the improvement loop

Inspired by Karpathy's autoresearch and Google's Harness², rebuilt small. One frozen test, one file the loop may edit, keep or discard.

Frozen tests

Walk forward through history. Using only events before year Y, predict sector direction after events in year Y. Score calibration. Plus 25 headlines: 15 real with known outcomes, 5 fake, 5 ambiguous.

Editable

engine/odds.py for matching, similarity and era weights. The prompts and skills. Nothing else.

Locked vault

Events from 2022 to 2026 never enter the loop. They're scored once, at the end, so we can say honestly how well it does on unseen history.

Phases

Tracks overlap on purpose. Each phase ends at a gate, a check that has to pass before work that depends on it starts.

P0 · Spikes and skeleton · H0 to H2
Fork zoya-harness into networth/, strip voice and Mac parts. Deploy a hello /chat on Maritime. Test Keiki tool calls and whether a notice keeps "8 of 11" word for word. Stand up the Telegram bot. Gate: a phone message reaches our code and our code can push one back.
P1 · Engine · H2 to H6
Curate events.csv with 4 agents (one for India) and a checker. Pull Nifty sector history and AMFI NAVs. Download French industry data. Build the backtest, episodes, baseline and eras. Gate: base-rate table exists, the leak test passes, the outside check reproduces pre-FOMC drift.
P2 · Watcher and triggers · H4 to H10
News pull, grouping, confirmation, price-first and news-first triggers, the rejection log. Gate: yesterday's real news produces the right confirmed stories, and fake ones are rejected.
P3 · Agents and delivery · H6 to H12
Spec files, 12 skills, composer, guardrails, onboarding with plan pick, the investing-day cron, tip check. Gate: a real brief built from real news arrives on a real phone.
P4 · Web view · H10 to H16
How am I doing, What if, Why card, landing, stats, from the Figma designs. Gate: every link opens on mobile, and every number matches the message it came from.
P5 · Evals and the loop · H14 to H18
25-headline eval, walk-forward test, then 4 to 6 improvement rounds. Gate: no unconfirmed story gets numbers, and every number traces to the engine.
P6 · Launch · H18 to H24
Bring in users, record the demo, write the submission, fix what breaks. Gate: 5 people outside the team onboarded, 3 replied to a brief unprompted, entry submitted.

Security and secrets

Rules
  • Secrets only in Maritime env vars, never in the repo.
  • Check Telegram's secret header and Keiki's request signature on every webhook.
  • Link tokens are signed, random, and can be rotated by texting "new link".
  • We store no holdings, account numbers or broker logins.
  • The stats page shows counts only.
  • Rate-limit /chat per user.
