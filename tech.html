Networth, how it's built

Every part we build, how it works, and the order we build it in over 24 hours. Read with the product spec. One rule runs through all of it: the engine computes every number, and the AI only writes words around them.

✓

Two kinds of model. Claude Code, on Opus 5.5 and Ankit's plan, does all build-time work. It writes the code, curates the event library, runs the backtests, and runs the improvement loop and the evals. OpenAI models run only the live product on Maritime, in three roles set in networth/config.toml. The cheap role (gpt-5.6-luna) groups news into stories. The judge role (gpt-5.6-sol) classifies each confirmed story once. The writer role (gpt-5.6-terra) words every user-facing message. Bedrock and Simbian are out. The product has to keep running when the laptop is closed and serve other people, so it can't sit on a personal Claude Code plan.

02

Architecture

One VM does the thinking and the talking. Telegram is the door, and Keiki is an optional second one. Everything the user sees traces back to the engine box.

Maritime VM

A Docker container that listens on $PORT, answers GET /health and POST /telegram/webhook, serves the web pages, and keeps everything in /data. Schedules registered with Maritime wake it for the news pull and the investing-day brief, because in-VM timers don't fire while it sleeps.

Telegram

Our server sends every message straight to Telegram through the Bot API, and Telegram posts each update to our webhook with a secret header we check. Keiki is not in this path. It is an optional extra surface, described under Delivery.

Offline

The backtest runs once at build time, then nightly. It reads the price store and the event library and writes the base-rate table. Nothing user-facing waits on it.

How one event becomes a million different messages

Shared, computed once per event. Base rates per (event type, asset) come from the engine and are cached. A million users don't mean a million backtests.

Filter per user. Drop any asset in exclude[] and any market not in markets[].

Order per user. The scoreboard is the same for everyone. What leads changes with the goal: a 3-year house goal hears about drops and recovery first, a 25-year horizon hears about long-run history.

Size per user. Ranges shown on their monthly_amount, in rupees or dollars, as illustrations. Their own plan is quoted back, never changed by us.

Compose per user. One writer-model call with the profile in context. At large scale the five lines become a template filled from the engine, and the model handles only chat. That keeps cost roughly flat per user.

✓

Why numbers_used exists. Every message records which engine values it quoted. The guardrail checks that every number in the text appears in that list. That is how "every call shows its work" becomes something the code enforces. The send gate is networth/guard.py, described under Agents and skills.

04

The engine

An event study, the standard method from finance research. For each past event, measure what each asset did against its own market (the S&P for US assets, the Nifty 50 for Indian ones) over a fixed window, then summarize honestly.

Rules it enforces
  • No peeking. An event after 4pm ET counts from the next session. A leak test fails the build if any return uses future data.
  • Episodes. Events within 21 trading days merge into one episode. Odds count episodes.
  • Baseline. Hit rate in 1,000 random windows of the same length, next to the event hit rate.
  • Two eras. Since 1926 from industry portfolios, since 1998 from ETFs. Both are shown when they disagree.
  • Weak signal under 8 episodes, in words and numbers.
The odds API
GET /tools/odds?type=oil_supply&asset=JETS
→ { n_episodes: 9, hit_rate: .73,
    baseline: .49, median: -.024,
    p10: -.09, p90: .03, era: "since_1998",
    episodes: [...] }

GET /tools/position_range
    ?user=u_1&ticker=JETS&type=oil_supply
→ { value: 2000, p10: -180,
    median: -48, p90: 60 }

Economic context

Phase 1B adds the economy as context. engine/macro.py downloads and caches FRED series into data/macro/ (USREC for recessions, CPIAUCSL for US inflation, FEDFUNDS and UNRATE) and the all-India CPI from MoSPI. The engine can then split one event type into two cases with split(). Existing odds() output is unchanged.

After Fed cuts

No recession followed compared with one did. This split sees the future, so every row is labelled hindsight: true. The product can show it as what happened in each case, never as a live signal.

After hikes

When inflation was high compared with low. It uses only data published before the event, with the CPI release lag, and a leak test fails the build if it doesn't. The cut-offs are fixed before any result is looked at.

!

The outside check. Before we trust any number, the engine must reproduce a published result. The candidate is Lucca and Moench (2015), who report that the S&P tends to rise in the 24 hours before scheduled Fed announcements. That's cited from memory, so step one is to confirm it in the paper. If our engine, run on SPY and real FOMC dates, can't show the effect, the engine has a bug.

05

News watcher and triggers

This replaces the old "paste a headline" idea. The system finds the stories itself.

Pull

Every 15 minutes in NSE hours (9:15 to 15:30 IST) and US hours, hourly otherwise. Exa news search over the last 48 hours with queries per event type and region. GDELT as backup.

Group and confirm

The cheap model (luna) groups articles into stories by title and summary. A story is confirmed with 3 or more original publishers in 24 hours, or 1 primary source such as the Fed, SEC, RBI, SEBI, PIB, NSE filings, OPEC or a company's investor page. Indian publishers include Economic Times, Mint, Business Standard and Moneycontrol. Wire reposts count once.

Trigger

Price first. An asset's excess return today beyond 2.5 times its 60-day volatility, then find the confirmed story behind it. News first. A confirmed story that classifies into a known type and cause.

✓

Measured on real news. One live cycle took 144 seconds and cost $0.30. It read 146 articles, built 75 stories, confirmed 33 and rejected 42. At 43 cycles a day, Exa is $4.52 a day and the models add an estimated $0.3 to $1.0. That is about $5 to $5.50 a day, or $150 to $165 a month, an estimate from two measured cycles.

Every story that fails a check is still logged with the reason. That log is the "honesty moment" in the demo, and it's how we tune the thresholds.

Spec-driven and skill-driven. An agent is a spec file. A skill is a folder with a SKILL.md. The harness turns a spec into a running agent with only the skills and tools it lists.

07

Request flows

The investing-day brief

Onboarding

A reply like "why?" or a forwarded tip

Telegram posts the update to /telegram/webhook. With the optional Keiki surface, Keiki pushes the turn to our server instead.

Jev picks one closed intent. The cheap model decides if Jev errors or times out.

chat.yaml runs with the skills for that intent. "Why" reads the stored Trail and never recomputes it.

Guardrails check the draft. The reply goes out with 2 or 3 suggested taps and, when useful, a link to the web view.

Explain this

The user forwards text, a link or a photo. A photo is fetched with Telegram's getFile and read by the vision-capable model in config.

The writer model says what it is, how big, the 3 or 4 things to know, and decodes the jargon, using only facts from what was sent or a confirmed source.

guard.py checks it with the sent content as its number source. It never says apply, skip, buy or sell, and it never gives a score or a rank.

The path · our own Telegram bot (Phase 3A)
  • Telegram posts each update to /telegram/webhook, and we check the secret header.
  • sendMessage with inline keyboards for every message. Forwarded tips arrive as normal messages.
  • send() is the only way out. It sends nothing to a stopped user and no proactive message to a paused one.
  • Works globally, no line cost. Users tap Start first.
Optional · Keiki external harness
  • Keiki owns the channel, inbound screening and traces.
  • It pushes each signed turn to our server.
  • We send the exact text with POST {turnUrl}/messages and complete the turn.
  • Time-boxed. A block over 30 minutes is recorded as a known gap, and Telegram ships alone.

Evals and the improvement loop

Inspired by Karpathy's autoresearch and Google's Harness², rebuilt small. One frozen test, one file the loop may edit, keep or discard.

Frozen tests

Walk forward through history. Using only events before year Y, predict sector direction after events in year Y. Score calibration. Plus 25 headlines: 15 real with known outcomes, 5 fake, 5 ambiguous.

Editable

engine/odds.py for matching, similarity and era weights. The prompts and skills. Nothing else.

Locked vault

Events from 2022 to 2026 never enter the loop. They're scored once, at the end, so we can say honestly how well it does on unseen history.

Phases

Tracks overlap on purpose. Each phase ends at a gate, a check that has to pass before work that depends on it starts.

P0 · Spikes and skeleton · H0 to H2
Fork zoya-harness into networth/, strip voice and Mac parts. Deploy a hello /health on Maritime. Stand up the Telegram bot. Gate: a phone message reaches our code and our code can push one back.
P1 · Engine · H2 to H6 · passed
Curate events.csv with 4 agents (one for India) and a checker. Pull Nifty sector history and AMFI NAVs. Download French industry data. Build the backtest, episodes, baseline and eras. Gate: base-rate table exists, the leak test passes, the outside check reproduces pre-FOMC drift.
P1B · Economic context for the engine
FRED series (USREC, CPIAUCSL, FEDFUNDS, UNRATE) and MoSPI all-India CPI, regimes fixed before any result is seen, and split(). Fed cuts with and without a recession (hindsight, never live). Hikes when inflation was high or low (only data published before the event). Gate: split() returns both sides with counts, baseline and the hindsight flag, and existing odds() output is byte-identical.
P2 · Watcher and triggers · H4 to H10 · passed
News pull, grouping, confirmation, price-first and news-first triggers, the rejection log, all on the OpenAI roles in config. Gate: yesterday's real news produces the right confirmed stories, and fake ones are rejected.
P3A · Server, Telegram and onboarding · passed, deployed
FastAPI server, the Telegram webhook, seven-question button onboarding with the plan pick, /plan, /pause, /resume, /stop and /help, a Docker image, and the Maritime deploy. Gate: a phone message reaches our code, onboarding completes, and /health answers on the public URL.
P3B · Brief, chat, Explain this, guard, Jev, Keiki
The investing-day brief composer, chat intents, Explain this, guard.py, Jev for the intent pick, per-user pages, rare alerts, Maritime schedules, and Keiki's external harness as a time-boxed stretch. Gate: a real brief built from real news arrives in Telegram, a fake tip is rejected with its reason, and a real IPO page comes back in plain words.
P4 · Web pages · passed
Why card, What if, Scoreboard, Track record, landing and stats, server-rendered from the engine. Gate: every link opens on mobile, and every number matches what the engine returns.
P5 · Evals and walk-forward calibration · H14 to H18
25-headline eval, walk-forward test, then 4 to 6 improvement rounds. Gate: no unconfirmed story gets numbers, and every number traces to the engine.
P6 · Launch · H18 to H24
Bring in users, record the demo, write the submission, fix what breaks. Gate: 5 people outside the team onboarded, 3 replied to a brief unprompted, entry submitted.

Security and secrets

Rules
  • The repo is public. Secrets only in Maritime env vars, never in the repo. No user data and no .env is committed, and data/ stays gitignored and out of the image.
  • Check Telegram's secret header and Keiki's request signature on every webhook.
  • Link tokens are signed, random, and can be rotated by texting "new link".
  • We store no holdings, account numbers or broker logins.
  • The stats page shows counts only.
  • Rate-limit chat per user.
