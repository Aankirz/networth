Networth · product spec v1 · Oct 24, 2026

Know what the news means for your money.

A calm friend in your messages who reads the world's news, checks it against 25 years of market history, and tells you only what touches what you own. Most days the message is short. Some days it says nothing needs your attention, and that is the point.

01 · What the books tell us to do

Four books, one rule each.

The Mom Test · Fitzpatrick

Ask people about what they did last time, not whether they would use your idea. Compliments are noise. Time, money and reputation are signal.

Talk to 5 real investors before H4. Count commitments, not compliments.

Don't Make Me Think · Krug

People scan, they don't read. Each screen should be obvious at a glance, and every question a user has to stop and answer costs you.

Line 1 of every message is the verdict for you. No typing without suggested replies.

Hooked · Eyal

Habits form from a loop of trigger, action, variable reward and investment. The loop must make the user's life better, or it is manipulation.

Hook on reassurance and understanding, never on trading.

Keep it simple

Every feature, channel and setting has a cost to the user and to us. Ship the smallest thing that delivers the core value, polished.

3 message types, 1 web page, 0 accounts, 0 passwords.

02 · The habit loop

Why someone opens this every morning.

Trigger

7am brief, and the worry itself

External: a message at the time they chose. Internal: the jolt of "is my money ok?" after a scary headline. Over time the internal one does the work. They see the news and think "let me check what Networth says".

Action

Glance at a text

The lowest effort action that exists. It arrives in iMessage or Telegram, an app they already open 50 times a day. No app to install, no login, no dashboard.

Variable reward

Relief, surprise, status

Some days "nothing you own is affected". Some days "this one matters, here's why". Often a surprising fact from history. And a card worth forwarding, which makes them look smart in the group chat.

Investment

Every reply makes it better

Adding a holding, saying "I care about oil", asking a follow up. Each one makes tomorrow's brief more personal. A weekly scorecard builds a track record they trust more each week.

The ethical line. Investing apps that hook people on action cause overtrading and losses. Ours hooks people on understanding. The most common honest message is "no action needed". If a feature would make people trade more, it doesn't ship.
03 · The journey

From stranger to daily habit in one minute.

Discover

A friend forwards an evidence card, like "What happened to airlines the last 11 times OPEC cut supply". The bottom of the card says "Get this every morning for what you own".

Feels like: that's interesting, and someone I trust sent it.

Start · 1 tap

The landing page has one sentence, a real sample brief, and three buttons. iMessage, WhatsApp and Telegram. Tapping one opens a chat with "start" already typed.

Feels like: no sign up, no risk.

Onboard · under 60 seconds

Two questions. "What do you own or watch?" They can type tickers, plain words like "tech and gold", or send a screenshot of their brokerage app. Then "When do you want your brief?" with buttons.

Feels like: that was easy. It understood my screenshot.

First value · immediately

Before the first 7am, they get "Here's what happened this week that touched your stuff". Time to first value is under a minute, not tomorrow.

Feels like: oh, it already knows something useful about my money.

Daily

A morning brief. A same-day alert only when something large hits what they hold, about a few times a month.

Feels like: I'm on top of it without trying.

Weekly

Sunday scorecard: what we flagged, what actually happened, including misses. Plus one question to deepen their profile.

Feels like: it's honest with me. I trust it more.

Share

Any message has a "forward this" card. The card is the growth engine. It carries the evidence and the invite.

Feels like: I look smart sending this.

05 · Message anatomy

Every message has the same five lines.

1

Verdict for you. "One thing matters for you today" or "Nothing you own needs attention." Readable in under 2 seconds.

2

What happened. One plain sentence. No jargon, or the jargon is explained in the same sentence.

3

What history says. N, how often, typical size. Always vs the S&P. Always says "small sample" under 8.

4

What it means for you. Usually calm. Says "not a reason to act" whenever true.

5

Next tap. 2 or 3 suggested replies, so they never wonder what to type.

Voice

  • A calm friend who reads filings for fun. Not a trader, not a hype account.
  • Short sentences. Under 400 characters before the first tap.
  • Never "buy" or "sell". It says "historically" and gives N.
  • Admits misses in public. The scorecard shows them.
  • Plain-language commands always work: "pause", "add TSLA", "fewer alerts", "stop".

07 · Keep it simple

What's in v1, and what we cut.

In v1

  • One agent on iMessage or SMS plus Telegram, via Keiki
  • Onboarding with screenshot parsing
  • Morning brief, rare alert, follow-up answers
  • Evidence card web page with share link
  • Landing page
  • Sunday scorecard, if time allows

Cut

  • Accounts, passwords, email login
  • A web dashboard or portfolio view
  • Brokerage account sync. A screenshot does the job.
  • Settings screens. Settings are things you type.
  • Payments
  • Push notifications from our own app. There is no app.
08 · Proof it works

Measure behavior, not compliments.

Mom Test interviews, first 4 hours

Five people who invest, 10 minutes each, in parallel with the build. Ask about the past only.

  • "Tell me about the last time news made you worry about your money. What did you do?"
  • "Where did you hear about it? How long after it happened?"
  • "Have you ever bought or sold because of a headline? How did it go?"
  • "What did you use to make sense of it? Google, ChatGPT, a friend?"
  • "How much time a week do you spend on this?"

Don't ask "would you use this?" A yes costs them nothing. Ask for their holdings and a time for the brief. That costs them something.
