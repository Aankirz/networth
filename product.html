01

Overview

Networth is three things working together. Most people only ever see the first one.

The habit

A morning text

At 7am, one short message. What happened, whether it touches you, and what history says. Most days it says nothing needs your attention.

The depth

A web view

One tap from any message. How your money is doing, how it would have done after events like today's, and the full reasoning behind every call.

The engine

History, counted

A library of past events and what each sector did after them. The engine makes every number. The AI only explains.

02

The problem

People earn well and invest, but they have no time to follow markets and no way to judge what a headline means for them.

What happens today
  • They copy what friends or influencers bought, and lose money. The founder did this for years.
  • Broker apps list strengths and risks, but never say how big they are or what usually happens next.
  • By the time a headline reaches their phone, the move has already happened.
  • ChatGPT gives confident answers with no track record and numbers it can't trace.
What they actually need
  • Someone to watch the world for them and stay quiet when nothing matters.
  • When something does matter, the plain-English version, sized to their money.
  • Honest odds from real history, with the sample size, so they can judge for themselves.
  • The reasoning, so they learn and trust it more over time.
03

Principles

Every design decision below traces back to one of these. When two conflict, the earlier one wins.

Messages first. The text is the product. The web view is depth for people who want it, never a place you must go.

We come to you. Nobody pastes headlines. Networth watches the news and decides what's worth your attention.

Every call shows its work. Each insight carries its reasons and its odds, and anyone can open the full trail. No black box.

The engine makes every number. The AI writes words. If a number in a message doesn't match the engine, the message doesn't send.

Calm over hype. "Nothing needs your attention" is a good message. We never push people to trade.

History, not instructions. We say what happened after similar events and what that range would mean for your money. The decision stays yours.

Your plan, the wealth manager's method

Private wealth managers start every client with a short written plan. It covers the goal, the timeline, the biggest drop the client can live with, and a split of the money into buckets. Networth helps you write yours in a two-minute chat, and then it holds you to it.

Safety

Money you'll need soon. Cash, T-bills, short bonds. You choose how many months it covers.

Core

Broad funds you hold for years. This is where most wealth gets built.

Growth

The ambitious part. Single stocks, sectors, crypto if you allow it. You decide how big it is.

You set the split with sliders. Networth replays your mix through every past episode, showing the worst year, how long recovery took, and the typical yearly range. You can compare it with well-known public mixes such as 60/40 or a target-date path. When the news pushes your real holdings away from your own plan, the brief says so: "Your Growth bucket is now 41%, above the 30% you set."

!

The line we don't cross. Telling one specific person "put $1k here and $2k there" is personalized investment advice. In the US it requires registering as an investment adviser. The publisher's exclusion that covers newsletters applies only to impersonal content (Lowe v. SEC, 1985, upheld for Seeking Alpha in 2024). So you choose the numbers, and we show you what history says about them. Registering later is a real path, since robo-advisors are registered advisers, but it isn't for this week.

!

India has its own rule. SEBI regulates research and recommendations on Indian securities through its Research Analyst rules, and it tightened the rules for financial influencers in 2024. This is cited from memory, so check it before launch. Networth stays on the safe side the same way it does in the US. It shows what history says, never says buy or sell, and labels every message as research, not advice. Get one short legal opinion before scaling in India.

05

How it works

The world produces endless news. Networth filters it down to the few things that matter to you, measured against history.

Watch

News from many outlets, all day, plus prices of every asset our users hold.

Confirm

A story counts only when 3 or more original publishers carry it, or a primary source like the Fed posts it.

Measure

Find past episodes of the same type and cause. Count what each sector did after them, compared with a normal period.

Rank

Keep only what touches your holdings, ordered by how much history says it matters. Usually 0 to 3 a day.

06

Showing our work

Every insight Networth gives comes with its reasons and its odds. Users can open the full trail, see every past episode behind a number, and estimate what the range would have meant for their own money.

What your money could do

History, sized to your position

For each holding an event touches, we apply the range of past outcomes to your actual position. For example: "On your $2,000 in JETS, the 11 past cases ranged from a $180 loss to a $60 gain over a month. The typical case was a $48 loss." Always labelled as history, never as a forecast.

Two numbers, always

Event odds next to normal odds

"Airlines trailed the S&P in 8 of 11 past cuts" means little alone. So we always add "in a random month, they trail about half the time." The gap between those two numbers is the real signal.

How we say odds in words

One fixed scale, so "usually" means the same thing every time. Words always sit next to the count.

We sayWhen history showsExample
Almost always90% or more of episodes"Almost always, 10 of 11"
Usually70% to 89%"Usually, 8 of 11"
More often than not56% to 69%"More often than not, 7 of 12"
Coin flip45% to 55%"A coin flip, 6 of 11"
Rarelyunder 30%"Rarely, 2 of 9"
Weak signalunder 8 independent episodes, any rate"Weak signal, only 6 past cases"
!

Where the line sits. We show reasons, odds and ranges for everyone in the same way, and the user decides. We never say "buy" or "sell". This keeps Networth on the research side of US rules on personal investment advice. That's our working understanding, not legal advice, and it needs a lawyer before launch.

Voice

Always
  • A calm friend who reads filings for fun.
  • Verdict in the first line, readable in 2 seconds.
  • Under 400 characters before the first tap.
  • Counts like "8 of 11" next to every word like "usually".
  • Admits misses in the report card.
Never
  • "Buy", "sell", "guaranteed", "can't lose".
  • A number the engine didn't produce.
  • Urgency to trade, countdowns, FOMO.
  • Jargon without a plain explanation in the same sentence.
  • More than one alert a day per holding.

  • The "Why?" reasoning trail
  • Dollar ranges on the user's own positions
  • How am I doing, What if, Why card pages
  • Landing page, live stats
Stretch
  • Your plan: buckets, sliders, history replay, drift nudges
  • Your own rules: "If VOO falls 15% from its high, remind me of my plan." You write the rule, we watch for it and show history.
  • Same-day alerts
  • Sunday report card
  • Fed calls split into recession and no-recession branches
Cut
  • Buy or sell calls
  • "Put $X here" allocations for a specific person
  • Futures, options, leverage, shorting. Never mentioned or promoted.
  • Accounts, passwords, payments
  • Broker linking (SnapTrade for the US, CAS or Account Aggregator for India). Parked until Ankit says to add it.
  • Settings screens. Settings are things you type.
  • iMessage. Telegram and WhatsApp cover launch.
13

Measuring it

Numbers we watch
  • Activation. Finished onboarding and received first value.
  • Brief reply rate. Share of briefs that get any reply or tap.
  • "Why?" rate. How often people open the reasoning. Trust in action.
  • Day 7 retention. Still not muted after a week.
  • Share rate. Cards forwarded per active user per week.
  • Mute rate. Early warning that we send too much.
Before we build, and before we submit
  • Five Mom Test conversations about what people did last time news scared them. Count commitments, not compliments.
  • The engine reproduces a published result before we trust it.
  • At least 5 people outside the team onboard, and 3 reply to a brief without being asked.

15

Open questions

What is the exact week-one cutoff, and what is the rubric?
Not published anywhere we could find. Ask at office hours. The answer decides where the last 6 hours go.
Does Keiki keep our numbers word for word?
Keiki's agent rewrites pushed notices in its own words. If "8 of 11" becomes "most of the time", briefs go out straight from Maritime through Telegram instead.
Which tagline?
"A personal wealth manager of yours" says the dream well, but it tells US users and regulators that we give personal advice. Safer options that keep the dream: "Think like your own wealth manager." "The research the rich pay for, now in your texts." "Know what the news means for your money." Ankit decides.
Do people prefer the text or the page?
Ask the five interviewees where they check their investments today. If most say a page, give the web view more weight.
